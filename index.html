Minimum scores

Criterion scores run 0 (poor) to 5 (excellent); the overall score runs 0 to 10. Underlined scores are your own overrides. Click a row for details, sources of each score, and to enter your own scores after a test ride.

How the overall score works

Each bike gets a 0–5 score on thirteen criteria, plus a yes/no for ABS that you can weight like the rest. Price, power, weight and tall-rider fit are computed from the numbers through the curves below; fit uses each bike's seat, peg and bar positions. The rest are researched judgements, which you can override per bike (open a row in the Compare tab). Test-ride access depends on whether you pick a new or used option.

The overall score, out of 10, is twice the weighted average of the criteria, using the weights from the Compare tab. With the weak-spot penalty turned up, it becomes a weighted power mean: a bike that scores 1 on something you care about can't make up for it elsewhere.


      
New or used

      
Each bike has one or more buying options, such as “New 2026” or “Used 2019–21”. The option changes the price, test-ride score, and sometimes reliability (older bikes lose a little) or specs (a different generation). By default each row shows whichever option scores best; pick another from the row’s drop-down, or change “Which option to score” above the table.

Where the numbers come from

      
Researched in September 2026. Treat every figure as an estimate to check, not a quote.

      
Prices

      
    
        
  • New: the UK on-the-road price (registration, first VED, plates, delivery and PDI included), from manufacturer UK sites and current dealer adverts. Where a brand quotes a price “plus OTR” (CFMOTO), about £200 is added. Where dealers are advertising below RRP, the price used is between the two.
    • 
        
  • Used: a typical dealer asking price for a tidy, average-mileage bike in that year range, from AutoTrader listings. Private sales usually run about 8% lower; tick “Used = private sale” to apply that.
    • 
        
  • Your own price for any bike (for example, a listing you found) can be entered in the bike’s detail panel.
    • 
      

      
Researched scores

      
Comfort, motorway manners, standing position, fun, reliability, running costs, luggage and green-laning scores come from UK and US reviews (MCN, Bennetts BikeSocial, Visordown, Cycle World, owner forums) and weigh what matters for a 6′4″ rider. Each score has a one-line reason in the bike’s detail panel. They are opinions: after a test ride, override them.

      
Test-ride access

      
New bikes: how close the nearest dealer is and whether the brand runs demo fleets (Honda, Yamaha, Triumph and BMW do; Aprilia, Moto Guzzi and CFMOTO demos are harder to find). Used bikes: how many are for sale, since common bikes are easy to find within a short drive, and most sellers allow a ride with cash or ID left as security.

      
Tall-rider fit

      
Knee, hip and forward-lean angles come from each bike's seat, footpeg and handlebar positions, for a rider 6′4″ (193cm) tall with a 34″ (86cm) inseam and slightly bent arms. Most are from the Motonomics simulator, recalculated with its own method. Seven older bikes it doesn't have (XL700V, TDM900, K75, F650GS Dakar, R1150GS, XRV750, VFR400R) come from cycle-ergo.com, converted to the same scale (its knee angles read about 2° higher). Geometry is for the standard seat and bars; the 2024-on F 800 GS has none, so its fit is a researched score.

      
Map and places

      
Coastline: Natural Earth. Postcode districts and towns: OS Code-Point Open via the Gibbs/uk-postcodes dataset (Open Government Licence). Locations are district centroids, accurate to a mile or two.
